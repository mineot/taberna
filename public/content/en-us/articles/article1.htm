When an idea finally comes to life

Not every beginning needs to be grand. Sometimes it only needs to fit into your next free afternoon.

Published by Helena Duarte on April 8, 2025.

For almost two years, I kept a blue notebook in the bottom drawer of my desk. It held lists, screen sketches, possible names, and questions I still did not know how to answer. The idea was simple: create a small exchange club that would bring together people with different skills. Someone would teach photography, someone else would explain how to care for a garden, and on another week everyone could simply talk about the books they were reading.

In the notebook, everything worked perfectly. There was a bright room, large tables, and a schedule planned months ahead. Outside its pages, I always found a reason to wait: I needed more time, more money, a better name. Preparing felt like work, but it was also a comfortable place to hide.

The first gathering

Things changed when I told Mauro, the owner of the neighborhood stationery store, about the idea. He listened to everything and asked why the first gathering needed a room. We could use the two tables on the sidewalk after closing time. The following Friday, I wrote a short invitation, sent it to a few friends, and stopped adding conditions.

Seven people came. We brought chairs from home, a thermos of coffee, and a cake that was a little too dry. The conversation planned for forty minutes lasted almost three hours. Before leaving, each person wrote down something they wanted to learn and something they could teach.

The project did not begin when it was ready. It began when other people could step into it.

Learning in public

We still make plans, but they are no longer a condition for taking action. Some gatherings are full; others bring together three people. We have changed the time, the format, and even the name. We discovered that most decisions could only be made after the project existed in the real world.

Starting small did not diminish the idea. On the contrary, it gave the project room to breathe, welcome opinions, and find paths I never would have imagined on my own. The old blue notebook is still in the drawer, but now it keeps a record of what happened, not only plans for what might happen someday.

If you are also waiting for the perfect circumstances, perhaps it is worth choosing a version of your idea that fits into this week. A conversation, a shared draft, or a gathering around a table can already be a beginning.
