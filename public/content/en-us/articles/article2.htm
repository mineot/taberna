May 14 — After the rain

An ordinary afternoon, the kind that seems to bring nothing new until we pay attention.

Wednesday, May 14, 7:42 p.m.

It rained all morning. The water tapped against the window so steadily that I postponed the alarm twice just to keep listening. When I finally got up, the street was empty and the trees looked greener than yesterday. I made too much coffee and left the second cup forgotten on the table until it went cold.

I spent much of the day trying to finish small tasks. I answered three messages, organized a shelf, and found, tucked inside a book, the ticket from the movie theater that closed last year. I do not remember which film we saw, but I remember the walk home and our debate over which apartment window would be the last to go dark.

At four, the rain stopped without warning. I went out to buy bread and decided to take the longer route. The sidewalks still shone, reflecting pieces of sky between the wires. In the square, two children pushed leaves into a tiny stream running beside the curb. A man waited for his dog to finish investigating every tree. No one seemed to be in a hurry.

I met Dona Lúcia near the newsstand. She was holding a yellow umbrella and a pot of basil. She said she bought it because the kitchen needed to smell like Sunday. I liked the phrase so much that I wrote it on the back of my shopping list. We spoke for only a few minutes, but she told me she was going to paint again. She had stopped a long time ago and had now chosen to begin with the hallway wall.

Maybe some changes arrive like this: carried under one arm on a wet afternoon, looking like nothing more than a plant or a can of paint.

I came home with the bread still warm. I opened the windows, and the apartment filled with the scent of wet earth. For the first time in weeks, I did not play music or turn on the television. I watched the light move across the table and realized how many things keep happening when I believe the day is standing still.

Tomorrow I want to buy a plant for the kitchen. I do not know if it will be basil. I just want something that reminds me to open the window after the rain.
